0–2 · Scenario

Say: “Same campus registration peak as on the slides. Demand is not steady.”

Listen for: students naming last year’s single-VM timeout.

Land: three one-liners (scalability / elasticity / availability) are reminders only — do not re-teach.

2–5 · Scale

Say: “Vertical or horizontal — defend it in one sentence.”

Listen for: “bigger machine” vs “more machines behind a balancer.”

Press if needed: “Where does session state live?” Horizontal without disposable replicas is a trap.

5–9 · LB & health

Say: “Toggle ‘fail health’ — what should the LB do?”

Listen for: drain / stop sending traffic, not “delete DNS.”

Land: health checks turn redundancy into availability. Prefer ready-for-traffic over “port open.”

9–14 · Autoscaling

Say: “Metric, min/desired/max, cooldown — that is the whole policy sketch.”

Listen for: why CPU might be a bad proxy during DB wait; why min ≥ 2.

Land: observe → decide → act; cooldown fights thrashing; max is a cost cap.

14–16 · Risk

Say: “One risk only — the one most likely in the twenty-minute peak.”

Gold answers: sticky/local state, single DB writer, thrashing, unbounded max.

16–19 · Reveal

Say: “Reasonable, not unique. Hold your summary next to the green box.”

Close with five ideas (below), then hand off to the AWS demo block if that is next on the agenda.

Possible answer key

  • Scale: horizontal scale-out of a preferably stateless web tier.
  • LB: in front of N identical instances; health check on /health meaning ready for traffic (deps OK), not only process listening.
  • Policy: RPS or latency preferred over CPU for this peak; min 2 / desired 2 / max capped; cooldown ~1–3 min so the open does not flap capacity.
  • Risk: ticket DB as bottleneck, or sticky sessions defeating scale-out — either is excellent.

Map to Week 2 takeaways

  • Scalability is about handling more load; elasticity is about adapting capacity as load changes. → Steps 1–2 & 4
  • Horizontal scale-out needs disposable, preferably stateless replicas behind a load balancer. → Steps 2–3
  • Health checks turn redundancy into availability. → Step 3 (fail toggle)
  • Autoscaling is a control loop: metric → policy → cooldown → min/desired/max (and a cost cap). → Step 4
  • Cloud APIs make this practical because capacity is a pooled, on-demand resource (NIST). → Bridge from lecture; demo shows mechanisms

Quick check (if you have 2 spare minutes)

  • Difference between scalability and elasticity in one sentence?
  • One reason horizontal scaling fails for a stateful app?
  • What does a load balancer use to stop sending traffic to a bad instance?
  • Three ingredients of an autoscaling policy?
  • Why is a single VM behind a public IP not enough for high availability?
